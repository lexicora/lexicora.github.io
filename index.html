Keep what you read.

Lexicora is an offline web clipper and bookmark manager in your browser's side panel. Capture a page while you read it, sort it into topics, and find it again in seconds.

Add to Chrome Firefox Edge

Free for non-commercial use · Version 1.1 · Source on GitHub

  • No accountNothing to sign up for
  • No serverNo requests of its own
  • No analyticsNothing about you leaves
  • Yours to takeMarkdown and JSON export

What is worth remembering is read once, then lost.

It sits in a tab, a docs site or an AI chat until it is gone. Lexicora keeps it while you are there, cleaned and structured, and finds it for you later. You browse exactly as before.

Features

01Capture

Save a page without leaving it.

The side panel sits beside the page you are reading. Capture it, and the article lands in an editor with its headings, lists, tables, code, callouts and images, and without the menus, sidebars and ads around it. It survives the page changing or disappearing, and it is yours to annotate.

Or bookmark it, and keep only what the page says about itself: title, link, site and description.

  • Side panel
  • Toolbar popup
  • Right-click menu
  • Keyboard shortcut
  • Just the selection

02Organise

Sort it your way.

Topics group what belongs together. Tags cut across them. Pin what you return to, archive what you are done with, favourite what matters.

The structure stays flat on purpose: topics hold entries, and nothing nests deeper, so there is never a folder to dig through.

03Find

Find it again in seconds.

Search runs over titles, tags, descriptions, site names and dates. Type site:react.dev to stay on one site, alone or with words after it. Press / anywhere in the panel to start.

And when you are back on a site, the side panel already shows what you saved from it.

04Keep

Take it with you.

Export a note, a topic or the whole library as Markdown, with front matter that Obsidian reads as properties. Or make a JSON backup and import it again, choosing what wins when something exists in both.

Your library is never locked in, and never anywhere but with you.

And the details

Small things, done properly.

  • A real editor

    Headings, lists, tables, code blocks with syntax highlighting, and callouts. Drag text and images in from the page you are reading.

  • Its own parser

    Pages are cleaned by Lexicora's own parser, down to what web components render in shadow DOM and code from embedded editors.

  • Find on the page

    ⌘ F or Ctrl F finds text in the side panel, the editor included. A heading map jumps through long entries.

  • Copy, formatted

    Copy an entry or a topic. It pastes formatted into rich editors and as Markdown into plain-text ones.

  • A gentle nudge

    After a while on a page, Lexicora can offer to capture it. Set the delay, or switch it off. In Chrome and Edge.

  • Light and dark

    Light, dark or your system's theme, painted correctly from the first frame.

Keyboard

Hands stay on the keys.

Three shortcuts work on any page. Inside the side panel, single keys do the rest: / searches, N starts a new entry, E edits, and ? lists every one of them.

Change any of the three in your browser's own extension shortcut settings.

Privacy

It is yours.

Lexicora keeps everything you capture on your own computer. It has no accounts, no servers and no analytics, and it sends nothing about you or your browsing anywhere.

Read the privacy policy
  • Your library is stored by your browser, on this device.
  • The extension makes no network requests of its own. Site icons and images in your entries load from where they are hosted, as they would on the page.
  • A page is read only when you capture or bookmark it, and only the page in front of you: never other tabs, never in the background.
  • Delete everything at once with Settings → Storage → “Clear all data”, or by removing the extension.
  • If a future version ever sends anything anywhere, the policy says so first, and the feature is yours to switch on.

Design principles

Opinionated on purpose.

Lexicora turns web content into searchable knowledge with as little friction as possible. It is not a note-taking app, a workspace or a social platform. Those are solved elsewhere, often at the cost of speed and clarity.

Capture-first
Saving should be effortless.
Retrieval-first
Finding it again must be instant.
Speed over complexity
No heavy UI, no deep nesting.
Flat by design
Intentional limits keep it clear.
Keyboard-driven
The mouse is optional.
Browser-native
It works where you read.
Local-first
The library lives on your device.
AI-optional
Useful without it. Version 1 has none.
